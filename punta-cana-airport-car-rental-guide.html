PUJ Airport Car Rental: Complete Arrival Guide

How the car rental area works at PUJ, estimated times, and the first routes from the airport.

Landing in a new country usually raises the same questions: where do I get my luggage, which way is the exit, where's my transport, and how do I find my reserved vehicle? If you're looking for information on PUJ airport car rental, this guide explains how to plan your arrival at Punta Cana International Airport and what to do once you receive your vehicle.

PUJ is the main tourist gateway to the eastern Dominican Republic and sits very close to Punta Cana, Cap Cana, and Bávaro. That means you can land and be driving toward your accommodation shortly after.

✈️ In short: allow 20-30 min for immigration, check where you'll pick up the car (inside the airport or coordinated with a local rental company), and send your flight number ahead of time for a smooth handover.

🛬 Arriving at Punta Cana International Airport

PUJ has Terminal A and Terminal B for international passengers. Your specific terminal will depend on your airline and flight.

After landing, the usual process is: immigration → baggage claim → customs → arrivals area → transport or vehicle handover.

The exact time is impossible to guarantee. With light congestion, getting through immigration can take about 20–30 minutes, but when several international flights land close together it can take considerably longer.

The same goes for luggage.

That's why, if a rental company is delivering your car at the airport, it's better to give them your flight number rather than just saying "I land at 3:00 PM." The plane may land at 3:00 PM and you might not walk out of the terminal until 3:40, 4:00, or later.

A useful detail for families: the airport itself has special assistance lines for families with children ages 0–3, as well as passengers with reduced mobility and other special needs.

🚘 How Does PUJ Airport Car Rental Work?

PUJ officially has rent-a-car services inside the airport. Operators listed in the official information include companies like Avis, Budget, Europcar, Hertz, Dollar, Thrifty, National, Enterprise, Alamo, and Sixt, spread across Terminals A and B.

But there's another common option in Punta Cana: booking with a local company and coordinating vehicle delivery right at the airport.

In that case, the process can look different.

Usually, before the trip you'll agree by WhatsApp on: flight number, terminal or meeting point, vehicle model, price, deposit, and required documentation.

When you exit the arrivals area, you meet the representative at the agreed spot and the handover takes place.

This avoids having to go to the hotel first and look for transport afterward.

⏱️ How Much Time Do You Need From Landing to Receiving the Car?

Don't plan the trip as if getting off the plane means you'll immediately be on the road.

You should account for:

  • Immigration: about 20–30 minutes under normal conditions, though it can take longer.
  • Baggage: usually a few more minutes of waiting, depending on the flight.
  • Customs and exit.
  • Meeting the representative.
  • Vehicle inspection and handover: about 10–20 minutes if the paperwork is already prepared.

In a smooth scenario, you can be ready fairly quickly. During peak hours, it's best to leave some margin.

Here's a simple advantage of setting up the booking before your flight: the contract, documents, and conditions can already be reviewed beforehand.

When you arrive, all that's left is identifying yourself, checking the car, confirming the fuel level, and getting the keys.

Before driving off, record a quick video of the vehicle's exterior and check for any existing damage.

🗺️ From PUJ Airport to Bávaro, Cap Cana, and Punta Cana

Once you're in the vehicle, the first routes are simple.

PUJ → Cap Cana: about 15–20 minutes depending on the destination. PUJ → Punta Cana Village: about 10 minutes. PUJ → Bávaro areas: usually around 20–30 minutes, depending on the exact location of your hotel. PUJ → Uvero Alto: about 40–50 minutes.

The main roads in the tourist area are relatively easy to follow with Google Maps or Waze.

If you've just arrived from a long flight, our recommendation is not to turn day one into a road trip.

Pick up the car, get to your accommodation, rest, and start exploring the next day.

Especially at night, driving on unfamiliar secondary roads requires more attention: you may encounter motorcycles, pedestrians, poorly lit vehicles, or hard-to-see speed bumps.

🔍 What to Check Before Leaving the Airport

Even if you're eager to get to the hotel, take five minutes to inspect the vehicle.

First, check the fuel level. Take a photo of the dashboard showing the level and mileage.

Then check:

  • Lights.
  • Air conditioning.
  • Tires.
  • Windshield.
  • Bumpers.
  • The sides of the vehicle.

Also confirm where the car's required documents are and save the company's contact number.

If you'll be using your phone as GPS, bring a car charger. It seems like a minor detail until you've spent three hours on navigation, music, and mobile data.

It's also a good idea to download an offline map of the area before traveling.

And a practical rule: never leave passports, cash, phones, or visible luggage inside the vehicle when you park it.

🧭 First Routes After Landing at PUJ

Punta Cana's location allows for several interesting getaways without having to cross the entire country.

One of the easiest is Cap Cana and Hoyo Azul. Depending on where you start, Hoyo Azul is about 20–25 minutes from Punta Cana.

Another option is Higüey, where you can see a Dominican city outside the resort environment.

For a full day, Bayahíbe is probably one of the most interesting routes. From Punta Cana it's about 65–70 km and around an hour's drive; many excursions to Saona Island leave from there.

Miches is about 1 hour and 30 minutes from Punta Cana and combines coast, mountains, and much less touristically developed landscapes.

And if you have more time, Santo Domingo turns your beach trip into a completely different kind of visit, especially thanks to the Colonial Zone.

You'll find concrete itineraries for organizing these trips in our article on the best road trip routes around the Dominican Republic.

🤔 Is It Worth Picking Up the Car Right When You Land?

For many travelers, yes — especially if you'll be using the vehicle for your entire stay.

Imagine a week in Bávaro. You need a transfer from PUJ to the hotel on arrival, another to the airport when you leave, and several taxis during your vacation.

Just the taxi between PUJ and Bávaro can run around US$35–45 per trip based on current references.

If you're already planning to rent a vehicle, receiving it on landing eliminates those first two transfers and lets you start using it from day one.

That said, it's not always necessary.

If you have an included transfer, plan to stay four days at a resort, and only want the car for one excursion, it may make more sense to receive it directly at the hotel for a day or two.

The best option depends on your itinerary, not a universal rule.

🚕 Taxis, Uber, and Other Transportation at PUJ

The airport maintains a list of authorized taxis and offers ground transportation services in its terminals.

Taxis are especially useful when you don't want to drive or just need to get to the hotel.

As a reference, a taxi between PUJ and Bávaro usually runs around US$35–45, while longer distances increase quickly: fares to Uvero Alto can run US$60–80, and to Santo Domingo around US$180–220.

That's why it's worth comparing the total mobility cost for your vacation, not just the first transfer.

For a couple planning to stay at the resort, a taxi or transfer may be enough.

For a family wanting to visit Bayahíbe, Cap Cana, Higüey, Miches, and several restaurants over a week, the math is completely different.
