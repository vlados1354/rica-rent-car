Road Trip Routes in the Dominican Republic: 5 Trips From Punta Cana

Five routes from Punta Cana with real distances, driving times and practical tips for each.

Punta Cana is famous for its resorts, but limiting your trip to the hotel zone means seeing only a small part of the country. Having a vehicle lets you discover towns, beaches, mountains, and cities that are all completely different from one another. These road trip routes in the Dominican Republic start from Punta Cana and are designed for travelers who want to use one or several days to explore on their own.

We've included approximate distances and times, but keep in mind they can change depending on your exact starting point, traffic, and stops.

🧭 General tip: check your fuel and tires before leaving, save your routes offline on Google Maps, and keep cash on hand for tolls and small expenses in less touristy areas.

1️⃣ Route 1: Punta Cana → Bayahíbe → Saona Island

Distance to Bayahíbe: about 65–70 km. Driving time: about 1 hour–1 hour 15 minutes each way. Recommended plan: full day.

This is probably one of the easiest getaways from Punta Cana.

The route mainly uses the Coral Highway and ends in Bayahíbe, a former fishing village turned into one of the main departure points for Saona Island.

The official Dominican tourism office places Bayahíbe about an hour from Punta Cana and identifies it as the main gateway to Saona, Catalina, and Cotubanamá National Park.

If your goal is Saona, the car obviously doesn't reach the island itself.

The idea is to drive to Bayahíbe and continue from there by boat or catamaran. The boat leg by speedboat usually takes about 30–45 minutes.

A reasonable plan would be: 7:00 AM departure from Punta Cana, 8:15 AM approximate arrival in Bayahíbe, 9:00 AM departure for Saona, a day on the island, 4:00–5:00 PM return to Bayahíbe, 6:00–7:00 PM return to Punta Cana.

If you don't want to visit Saona, Bayahíbe is also worth a trip on its own. You can combine Playa Dominicus, local restaurants, and a visit to Altos de Chavón in the La Romana area.

2️⃣ Route 2: Punta Cana → Santo Domingo and the Colonial Zone

Distance: about 190–200 km. Driving time: around 2 hours 30 minutes each way. Recommended plan: leave early and return in the afternoon/evening.

This is the route that most changes the image many tourists have of the Dominican Republic.

You leave a mainly touristic destination and arrive at a Caribbean capital with over five hundred years of history.

The main road is good: the network connecting Punta Cana with La Romana and Santo Domingo includes the Coral Highway and the main routes in the eastern part of the country. The Dominican tourism office notes that this connection allows travel between Santo Domingo and Punta Cana in under three hours.

Your main target should be the Colonial Zone. Here you can walk Calle El Conde and the surrounding streets, visit the Cathedral of the Americas, Plaza de España, the Alcázar de Colón, and stroll through streets that contrast completely with Bávaro.

A practical itinerary: 6:30 AM departure from Punta Cana, 9:00 AM arrival in Santo Domingo, 9:30 AM–2:00 PM Colonial Zone, 2:00 PM lunch, 3:30–5:00 PM the Malecón or another part of the city, 5:00–6:00 PM heading back.

We don't recommend leaving too late if it's your first time driving around the country.

You should also factor in fuel, possible tolls, and traffic entering or leaving Santo Domingo. It's a long day, but entirely doable.

3️⃣ Route 3: Punta Cana → Cap Cana → Hoyo Azul

Distance: about 20 km from the Punta Cana area, depending on your starting point. Time: around 20–25 minutes. Recommended plan: half day or a relaxed day.

Not every road trip route in the Dominican Republic needs to cover hundreds of kilometers.

If you want a simple outing, especially on your first or last day, Cap Cana is a great option.

Hoyo Azul is about 19 km by road from certain points in Punta Cana, with an estimated time close to 24 minutes.

The area lets you combine different experiences without spending hours driving.

You can start early with Hoyo Azul and then continue to Cap Cana's beaches or restaurants. Another option is to spend the afternoon at Marina Cap Cana and head back to Bávaro for dinner.

It's also a good first route if you've just picked up a vehicle and are still getting used to driving in the Dominican Republic.

If you're arriving that same day, check out our PUJ airport and car rental guide first: Cap Cana is very close to the airport and can easily be worked into the start or end of a trip.

4️⃣ Route 4: Punta Cana → Miches → Montaña Redonda → Playa Esmeralda

Distance to Miches: about 100 km. Time: about 1 hour 30 minutes each way. Recommended plan: full day.

This is one of our favorite routes for anyone who wants to see a different side of the Dominican Republic.

Miches sits on the northeastern coast and still preserves large areas with far less development than Bávaro.

The official tourism office places Miches about 1.5 hours from Punta Cana. Its main attractions include Montaña Redonda, Playa Esmeralda, and the area's lagoons.

A possible route: 7:30 AM departure from Punta Cana, 9:00 AM Montaña Redonda, 11:30 AM departure for Miches, 12:00–1:00 PM lunch, 2:00 PM Playa Esmeralda, 4:30–5:00 PM heading back, arriving in Punta Cana around 6:30 PM.

Montaña Redonda is known for its 360-degree panoramic views of the coast, Laguna Limón, and the region's landscape.

This is where having your own vehicle really pays off. The attractions are spread out, and the freedom to adjust your itinerary is worth more here than in a destination where everything is packed into a few streets.

Bring water, sunscreen, and some cash. The tourism office itself recommends cash for some of Miches's local attractions.

5️⃣ Route 5: Punta Cana → Samaná: Spectacular, but Not a Quick Day Trip

Distance: about 280–310 km depending on the exact destination. Time: about 4–5 hours each way. Recommended plan: at least one night.

Samaná had to be on this list, but we also need to be honest: we don't recommend Punta Cana–Samaná–Punta Cana as a normal day trip.

Current estimates put the drive at around four hours or more each way, depending on the route and the exact point on the peninsula. That means eight or nine hours of driving just for the round trip.

Can it technically be done? Yes. Is it the best way to enjoy Samaná? No.

The ideal approach is to turn it into a short one- or two-night road trip.

You can leave Punta Cana early, arrive in the afternoon, and choose Samaná, Las Terrenas, or Las Galeras as your base.

The peninsula deserves time: beaches, mountains, waterfalls, and landscapes very different from the eastern tip of the country.

Depending on the season, Samaná is also internationally known for humpback whale watching.

With one overnight stay, the trip stops being a race against the clock and becomes a real road trip.

💡 Tips Before Taking a Road Trip Around the Dominican Republic

Before any long drive, check your fuel and tires.

Use Google Maps or Waze, but don't blindly follow every shortcut. For long distances it's better to stick to main roads even if the app shows a slightly faster secondary route.

Avoid starting an unfamiliar route too late in the day.

On secondary roads you may encounter motorcycles, pedestrians, animals, or poorly marked speed bumps.

Keep some cash on hand for small expenses and tolls.

And if you're still looking for a vehicle, first check out how to rent a car in Punta Cana and confirm that the contract allows the trips you want to make.

For Bayahíbe, Santo Domingo, or Miches you don't necessarily need an SUV. An economy sedan in good condition is perfectly enough for most of the main routes.

For four or five passengers with a lot of luggage, or if you want more height and comfort, an SUV may make more sense.

🌴 The Dominican Republic Changes Once You Leave Punta Cana

Punta Cana is an excellent base, but the country doesn't end at Bávaro.

In just over an hour you can be walking around Bayahíbe or looking out over the coast from Montaña Redonda. In a few hours you can go from a resort beach to the historic streets of Santo Domingo. And with one or two extra nights, you can turn Samaná into one of the best road trips of your whole vacation.
